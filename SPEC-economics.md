# Economics

Status. This document is normative. It sits under the crypto policy. If anything conflicts with that policy, stop and report.

This document defines the native asset, its issuance, the rewards, the fee flow, . Every number here is a starting value set at genesis and changed only through the Chain upgrades track of governance.

## The native asset

The native asset is QTOV. It pays fees, it is staked by validators, and it is the unit of the treasury. New supply is created only through the single mint path in governance, the Mint QTOV track, and through staking rewards once the staking pool is exhausted. The mint path carries a yearly ceiling of two percent of the total supply, and never less than 100,000 QTOV, and a referendum that would mint above it is refused, so newly raised capital or genuine network need can be met through a high threshold public vote without unbounded dilution. No single key can mint, only a passed referendum can.

## Validator rewards

A validator earns a reward for the work it performs in each epoch it is on the committee, which is attesting and proposing, exactly the work the protocol can see and count. A validator is never paid for being offline, no work earns no reward. A validator's share is set by its stake and by whether it did its duty in the epoch, and explicitly not by transaction volume. Paying a validator per transaction is prohibited, because it would let a validator spam the chain to inflate its own wages, and the protocol does not do it. The fee share is the only place transaction volume ever touches validator income, and it reaches a validator only as the ten percent proposer share of the fee split defined below, paid to the account of the block proposer of the round. The session reward is paid from the 685,714 QTOV staking pool first, and only once that pool is exhausted is the remainder newly issued. The validators reward pool is never funded from fees. A validator's own reward for a session is bounded two ways at once. It is driven by its stake and by whether it did its duty, at a rate that itself steps up when network activity is high, and the total paid to all validators in a session of 182 days is capped at five percent of the total supply. That session cap is a real, enforced limit, not a target. What is not fixed is a single percentage yield for the network as a whole. The staking pool that funds each session's rewards first is a fixed allocation set at genesis, and the network level yield is the session emission divided by the total staked, so it falls as the staked ratio rises and never exceeds the five percent session cap. The slashing rule is decided and set out in the validator economics under permissionless section below.

Rewards accrue per session and each one unlocks 365 days after it was earned, never on a fixed calendar date, because a fixed date would pay a validator that bonded last week on the same schedule as one that served all year. An operator sees what it has earned accrue visibly per session, but each reward does not reach the market until its own 365 days have passed. This is the sell pressure control, earned value is real and visible from the first session but is released only 365 days after it was earned, and it works alongside the five percent session cap rather than instead of it.

## Validator economics under permissionless validation

This section is frozen. It is decided design in force, in the same sense as QORUS itself, and it is not reopened without the founder. The slashing model, the exit rules, the bond lock, the unbonding, the reward accrual and its unlock, and the emergent yield are all frozen and set out below. The issuance source is decided as well, the staking pool first and then new issuance under the five percent session cap, and rewards go to validators only, so there is no delegation tier. The genesis supply is 4,571,429 QTOV, of which the fifteen percent staking and network reward allocation is 685,714 QTOV. That starting figure is not a fixed supply. The single mint path in governance can raise it by a passed vote within a yearly ceiling of two percent of supply, never less than 100,000 QTOV, and staking rewards can add new issuance once the staking pool is exhausted. Nothing has minted since genesis, so today the number has only ever moved down, through the fee burn.

The slashing model, decided. The principle is maximum severity where a fault proves malice and no penalty at all where it proves only misfortune. An attributable fault cannot happen by accident and is provable on chain, so it carries everything. A liveness fault is a power cut, a network blip, or a cloud outage, so punishing it at all would destroy the validator set without deterring a single attacker. This is a protocol enforcement from on chain evidence, and it is separate from the Freeze and asset recovery track of governance, which the second constitutional invariant forbids from ever touching validator stake.

Attributable faults burn one hundred percent of the bond with a permanent ban, no exceptions. They are two draws revealed for one slot from one account, a preimage used out of its position, double signing or equivocation at any height, a long range attack, bridge attestation fraud, and a coordinated or cartel attack, in which every participant loses their whole bond and is banned. An honest node cannot produce any of these through bad luck. This is what makes the bond cost of the one time key sortition real. A second draw costs a second full bond, and abusing it forfeits that bond entirely, against a gain of at most one seat among about five hundred worth a single attestation. The penalty exceeds the gain by orders of magnitude, and that is the intent.

Liveness faults are never slashed. Downtime, missed slots, a power cut, or a cloud region outage costs a validator nothing from its bond, so an isolated operator whose machine died and a mass outage caused by a provider rather than an attacker both leave the stake intact. Only an attributable fault touches the bond.

Slashed stake is burned, removed from the total supply, and forfeited bonds and deposits go to the governance controlled treasury. Neither is ever taken by a company or redistributed to other validators, because redistribution would pay the set for a peer's fault and invite exactly the coordination the slashing exists to deter. This is the protocol direction and it holds regardless of any other wording elsewhere.

Withholding carries no penalty and is recorded as an accepted residual. An account that draws, sees an unfavourable output, and reveals nothing is invisible and unpunishable, but it forfeits a seat rather than gaining one, so it is not an attack.

Exiting is a right and never a fault. A validator that requests to unbond or leaves the set is not slashed for it. Slashing an exit would be capital entrapment, and it would deter honest staking far more than it deters an attacker who was always prepared to lose the bond. The defence against a validator that bolts to escape a slash is not a penalty on leaving, it is the unbonding period, during which the stake stays slashable so a fault discovered after the request still lands.

A validator that completes ninety days and exits forfeits nothing and keeps every reward it worked for, which continues to unlock 365 days after each reward was earned, even after the exit. Leaving after a completed lock is an honest act, and punishing it by slashing the bond or forfeiting accrued rewards would be capital entrapment that deters honest operators far more than an attacker who was always prepared to lose the bond.

The bond lock is ninety days, and it is a deliberate departure from the majors, stated here as a design choice with its cost named. From bonding, the protocol refuses any unbond request for ninety days. This is not a penalty and nothing is slashed, the request simply fails. This is the commitment mechanism. After the ninety days elapse an exit request starts the twenty one day unbonding below, so the earliest possible exit is one hundred and eleven days from bonding. That is roughly fifteen to thirty five times Ethereum's three to seven days and Solana's two to three, and longer than Cosmos at twenty one days, the strictest major, so it is a real deterrent to mercenary capital while staying far short of freezing capital for a year.

A twelve month lock was considered and rejected, recorded here so it is not reopened casually. Professional operators price the risk of frozen capital, so a lock that extreme raises the yield they demand and settles the network at roughly twenty to twenty five percent staked earning five to six percent, outside the target band. Fewer validators earning more is the opposite of the intent. Ninety days with the rolling reward unlock secures commitment without that cost, so one hundred and eleven days is the deliberate departure and it is enough.

After the ninety day lock ends, a validator that requests exit enters a twenty one day unbonding period during which the stake remains fully slashable, so an attributable fault discovered after the request still reaches the bond. Twenty one days was checked against the time evidence takes to surface, not assumed. The base unbonding stays at twenty one days for every validator and is not stretched to the slowest bridge, because stretching it would punish the whole set for the slowest corridor. It is adequate for the faults whose evidence is on chain when they happen, the double draw, the out of position preimage, equivocation, and a cartel whose coordinated blocks and votes are recorded at the time.

The frozen model's slashing coverage is adequate for those on chain immediate faults only, and this limit is stated plainly so the frozen marker is not read as covering more. Two attributable faults are open and are not closed by the twenty one day unbonding. A long range attack cannot be bounded by any unbonding period at all, because its evidence surfaces whenever the attacker chooses to reveal the alternate history, so it needs a weak subjectivity checkpoint, which is a consensus model addition brought as a short options paper for the founder and not wired in here. Bridge attestation fraud surfaces only when the slowest bridged corridor finalises or its dispute window closes, which can exceed twenty one days, and it is handled below as a separate bonded role rather than by changing the base model.

Bridge attestation is a separate bonded role. A validator that attests to a bridge corridor bonds separately for that role, and that bridge exposed stake carries its own unbonding of at least the slowest corridor's finality plus its dispute window, longer than the base twenty one days. Only validators that take the bridge role carry it, so the base validator is never penalised for a bridge they do not serve. This does not gate testnet, since the bridge is post testnet, and the corridor grades that set the period are in the gateway specification.

Rewards go to validators only. There is no commission and no delegator share, because no holder earns from a validator's work without bonding and running as a validator itself.

The remaining validator parameters are confirmed and frozen. The minimum self stake is 2,000 QTOV, and it is load bearing for the leadership neutrality proof in the sortition, not only for the break even economics. That proof is exact only because every account's stake fraction stays above the floating point cliff in the leader score, and the 2,000 QTOV floor is what holds it there, about two to the forty two above the cliff at full supply, so lowering the minimum to widen the set would silently weaken the security proof. A change to this floor forces a re check of the leadership neutrality proof in the sortition, and a below floor account is rejected from the committee by an eligibility conformance vector. This cross binding is stated the other way in SPEC-sortition-onetime.md, so a floor change cannot pass through either document unnoticed. Admission is permissionless with stake as the only barrier and a deterministic stake ranked active set. The epoch is twenty four hours. Slashing is exactly as decided above, attributable faults at one hundred percent of the bond with a permanent ban, no liveness or downtime slashing at all, withholding an unfavourable draw an accepted residual, and all slashed stake burned.

The yield mechanism is decided. The network level yield is emergent rather than a fixed target, the pot divided by the total staked, and it finds its own level as participation rises and falls, the way Ethereum, Solana, Avalanche, and Cosmos each let their own yield curve move with participation. This is distinct from the session cap above, which is a real enforced limit of five percent of the total supply on what all validators together can be paid in one session. Leaving the network level yield to find its own level, rather than fixing it low, is the fix for the failure the earlier draft named, that a fixed low target left the permissionless set to the organization and to holders with a non economic reason to run a node. The source that fills the pot is decided as well, the staking pool first and then new issuance under that cap.

The staking model for holders. Rewards go to validators only. A holder who wants to earn on QTOV bonds at least 2,000 QTOV and runs as a validator, and there is no delegation or pooling tier that shares a validator's reward.

The issuance source is decided. Each session's rewards are paid from the 685,714 QTOV staking pool first, and only once that pool is exhausted is the remainder newly issued, with the total paid in any session capped at five percent of the total supply. The three models once costed in OPTIONS-validator-economics.md are kept there as history and are not the rule. The fee split is no longer open. It is the founder decided seventy burn, ten proposer, and twenty grants distribution set out under Fees, burn, and split below, and it is implemented and in force.

## Validator economics conformance

These vectors are frozen before the staking code that implements them, in the workspace pattern where a hostile or a required vector is written before the feature it gates. They live in the Quantova Conformance repository and become executable negative and positive tests when the staking module is built. Four are mandatory. An unbond request before ninety days from bonding is refused by the protocol, the request fails and nothing is slashed. An attributable fault committed or discovered during the twenty one day unbonding still slashes the exiting stake in full. A reward accrues only for an epoch the validator actually worked, an epoch missed or spent offline accrues nothing, and the reward is never a function of transaction volume. A reward unlocks 365 days after the day it was earned, not on a fixed calendar date, so a reward earned later unlocks later. Each of these is a frozen requirement of the model above.

## The prover auction

Provers do the heavy work of execution, signature verification, and proving, and they are paid from fees through an open auction. A prover bids to serve work and earns the fee for the work it completes. A prover holds a liveness bond that is forfeited if it accepts work and fails to deliver. A prover holds no vote.

## Fees, burn, and split

A transaction pays a fee in the native asset. The floating fee stays exactly as designed, a band from five hundredths of a cent to one tenth of a cent that floats in QTOV through the governance rate and is paid at what the sender bids. Only the split of the collected fee is set here, and this is the founder decided distribution and it is in force.

Every fee divides three ways. Seventy percent is burned. Ten percent goes to the validators of the round, which is the account of the block proposer that produced the block. Twenty percent goes to the grants account. The burn is an Ethereum base fee style destruction. The seventy percent is removed from the total supply, so the supply falls with every fee and tracks the activity on the chain rather than a fixed schedule. The sender pays the whole fee, the proposer share and the grants share are the only parts credited, and the burned share is destroyed, so the sum of balances and the total supply both fall by the burn and stay equal. The burn and proposer shares are computed by flooring and the grants share is the remainder, so any rounding dust falls deterministically into the grants share and no unit is ever created. The three shares sum to the fee exactly, which the code proves with a conservation test. The shares are changed only through governance.

The grants account is the keyless governance grants account, a public address that holds no key and is spendable only through a governance vote. Marketing and the market maker are funded from the genesis reserves and take no cut of any fee, so they are not part of the fee path.

The validator share is a direct credit to the round's proposer and not a payment into the validators reward pool. It is the fee flow reaching validator income, and it is still not a payment per transaction to any one validator beyond the single proposer of the block. The validators reward pool is the 685,714 QTOV staking pool, followed by new issuance under the five percent session cap once it is exhausted, and it is never funded from fees.

There is no yearly total supply burn. The per fee seventy percent burn and the burn of a double signing validator's slashed bond are the only burns on the chain.

## Delegation

There is no delegation tier. Rewards go to validators only, so a holder who wants to earn on QTOV bonds at least 2,000 QTOV and runs a validator.

## The fee model

The fee is defined in United States dollars and charged in the native asset. Every fee, measured in dollar value, falls within a band from five hundredths of a cent to one tenth of a cent, which is USD 0.0005 to USD 0.0010, and it never exceeds one tenth of a cent whatever the price of the native asset. This band is set to undercut the incumbent low fee chains so the chain is attractive for stablecoin settlement.

## How a fee is computed

The fee schedule is stated in dollar micro units, not in the native asset. A simple transfer sits at the low end of the band, and a heavier transaction scales up within the band but never past the one tenth of a cent ceiling. At the time a fee is charged it is converted to the native asset by a protocol parameter, the rate of the native asset in dollars. The fee in the native asset is the fee in dollars divided by that rate.

## The rate parameter

The rate of the native asset in dollars is a protocol parameter updated through governance as a parameter change on the Chain upgrades track, on a controlled cadence and within a bounded step size per update. It is never read from a live external price feed in the transaction hot path, because a live feed in consensus is a manipulation risk and a liveness risk, and it is prohibited.

## The ceiling is a native invariant, not a dollar one

The fee is capped in QTOV and targeted in USD. The hard runtime invariant is a native ceiling, a maximum number of base units a fee can ever be, set in genesis and independent of the rate, refused above the way an over ceiling mint is refused, so no rate however stale drives a fee past it. The one tenth of a cent is the dollar target the governance rate is held to, not a runtime dollar cap, because a runtime dollar cap would require reading the true price of the native asset in the hot path, which the rate parameter section prohibits as a manipulation and a liveness risk.

So the honest claim is that a fee is capped in QTOV, targeted in USD, and the peg is maintained by governance keeping the rate current. If the native asset rises faster than governance re-pegs, the realized dollar cost drifts above the target, because a fixed native ceiling is worth more in dollars at a price the chain does not observe. That residual is inherent to a chain with no price feed and is accepted deliberately. The chain does not claim that a fee can never exceed a tenth of a cent, because that sentence requires the feed the chain refuses.

## Fee edge cases

When the rate is stale the fee is computed from the last known rate and bounded by the native ceiling, so it can drift cheaper than the target and it can drift dearer than the target, and it can never exceed the native ceiling in base units. When the native asset rises and governance keeps the rate current, the fee becomes a smaller amount of the native asset while the target still holds, so a user pays less. When the native asset rises faster than governance re-pegs, the native ceiling holds the base units while the dollar value drifts up, the residual named above. When the native asset falls sharply, the fee stays in the band and the congestion component handles spam separately. A fee never rounds to zero, since the five hundredths of a cent floor keeps spam paying something.

## The congestion component

There is a congestion component that is zero under normal load and rises only when block capacity is genuinely contended. It exists only to price out a flood. It is not a permanent priority fee market, and it must not inflate the everyday cost the way priority fees do on other chains. Cheap fees come from abundant capacity, not from removing pricing.

## Fee reconciliation

The fee is split seventy percent burned, ten percent to the round's proposer, and twenty percent to the grants account, and the congestion component follows the same split. None of this changes the single mint path of the native asset, since the fee is a charge in the native asset and not an issuance of it. The fee burn and the slashing burn are the only places supply falls, and they fall through the same debit that keeps the total supply equal to the sum of balances. Supply can also rise, but only through a passed Mint QTOV referendum within its yearly ceiling or through staking rewards issued under the session cap once the staking pool is exhausted, never through a fee.
