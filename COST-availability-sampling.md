# Costing the availability sampling path

Status. This is the analysis behind a decision, not normative and not an implementation. It is the honest costing of the only path that takes the per validator floor well below one gigabit, the path where most validators stop executing every transaction. It costs the two things a non executing validator must replace, validity and availability, with real numbers where they are measured and labelled estimates where they are not, and it says plainly which half is affordable and which is not. It builds nothing. The reference load is the target, a hundred thousand transactions a second over 150 millisecond slots, which is 15,000 transactions a block at about 3.5 kilobytes each, a block of about 52 megabytes.

Decision. The founder decided to hold for proven validity. Byzantine trusted validity is not taken, universal re execution and the one third threshold are kept, and the slower chain is accepted, because one honest re executor protecting validity absolutely, so that a fully corrupt committee can halt the chain but cannot lie to it, is worth more than the latency and most chains do not have it. The reasoning and the conditional are recorded in SPEC-consensus-qorus.md. Proven validity is not affordable today, so the trustless throughput path is a multi year trajectory the chain is not on.

## What a non executing validator gives up, and must replace

A validator that executes every transaction gets two guarantees for free from the act of executing. It knows the block is valid, because it applied the transactions itself and checked the state root. And it holds the data, because it downloaded it. A validator that stops executing loses both and must obtain each another way. Validity must come from somewhere, and availability must come from somewhere. This paper costs each. The download saving is real only if both are replaced safely, because a validator that trusts a block it did not check and cannot reconstruct is not securing anything.

## What the certificate binds today

The certificate binds that a supermajority of the committee attested, and after the succinct certificate work, that their module lattice signatures verify over the exact decision preimage, the block, the committee, and the count. It binds attestation. It does not bind validity, and it does not bind availability. A validator that holds the certificate knows a supermajority signed, and nothing more by cryptography. Everything below is about what would have to be added to that.

## Validity, the first way, byzantine trusted

Under the assumption the protocol already makes, fewer than one third of a committee is faulty, a supermajority attestation is itself evidence of validity, because an invalid block cannot gather two thirds of honest attestations. So a non executing validator can infer validity from the certificate it already has, at essentially zero added compute. This is the cheap answer and it is real.

The cost is not compute, it is the loss of independent re execution. Today many independent validators re execute every block, so a committee that breaches the one third assumption for a single block is caught by everyone else who checked. If most validators stop executing, that second line is gone, and the chain's validity rests entirely on the committee of that one block meeting the one third assumption, with no independent check behind it. That is not a new trust assumption, it is the same one the chain already makes for safety, but it removes the redundancy that made a one third breach survivable. Real number, marginal validator cost near zero. Real cost, the independent re execution check is given up, and a bad committee is no longer caught by the rest of the set.

## Validity, the second way, proven, and this is the expensive half

A validity proof makes it trustless. A STARK proves that applying the block's transactions to the old state root yields the new state root, and a non executing validator that verifies the proof knows the block is valid without trusting the committee at all. This is the honest, trustless version, and it is the one that is not affordable at the target today.

Here is the honest state of the stack. What is measured is the certificate proof, the aggregation of the committee's signature verification, at about 31 seconds to prove, 82 milliseconds to verify, and 2.75 megabytes, from the succinct certificate work. That proves that five hundred signatures verify. A full execution validity proof is a categorically larger object, it must arithmetize every virtual machine step of every transaction in the block, not five hundred signature checks but fifteen thousand transactions each running thousands of machine steps. The stack does not have a measured execution proof, so every number in this section is a labelled estimate from what execution proving costs in general, not a measurement, and it should be read as the erasure coding caveat was read, as a projection and not a fact.

The estimate. Fifteen thousand transactions at very roughly ten thousand virtual machine steps each is on the order of one hundred and fifty million steps a block. A hash based STARK prover proves somewhere between about one and ten million steps a second on one strong machine, so one block is between about fifteen and one hundred and fifty seconds of proving on a single machine. To keep pace with a 150 millisecond slot that is between about one hundred and one thousand machines proving continuously, a real prover farm. The verify side is cheap, tens of milliseconds like the certificate, so the non executing validator's own cost is small, it is the proving that is heavy and it sits with the permissionless provers who are paid from fees. At the target the fee flow is large enough to fund such a farm in principle, a block of fifteen thousand transactions at a tenth of a cent is a few tens of dollars, and a farm of hundreds of machines is a few million dollars a year against a fee flow that at target is far larger. So the money is not the blocker. The blocker is two things, that the proving throughput to do this per block at the target does not exist on affordable hardware today, it is the frontier of the field, and that the proof arrives seconds after the block, so a validator that waits for the proof to finalise adds seconds of latency, which breaks the sub second finality unless the proof is treated as a slower backstop behind byzantine trusted finality rather than the thing finality waits on.

There is a further constraint that is ours specifically. The proving must be hash based and post quantum, no pairing and no elliptic curve commitment, which is the same STARK family the certificate uses, so the estimate above is for the construction we are allowed to use and not for the cheaper pairing based proofs other chains lean on. That is a real cost of the crypto law and it is named here.

So proven validity is trustless and it is not affordable at the target today. It is a multi year hardware and research trajectory, the stack has the verify side and not the proving side, and even when the proving farm exists the proof lags the block by seconds. Said as plainly as the erasure coding result, trustless validity at a hundred thousand transactions a second is not affordable now.

## Availability, data availability sampling, and this is the affordable half

Availability is the half that works. Erasure code the block, commit the shards under the header, and each node samples a few random shards and checks them against the commitment. If the samples are present, the whole block is reconstructable with high probability, so a node confirms the data is held without downloading it.

The number is good. A node that samples about thirty shards catches a withheld block with probability better than about one in a thousand, and about seventy five samples takes that past one in a billion. Each sample is a shard cell of roughly half a kilobyte plus a Merkle path of roughly half a kilobyte to prove it sits under the header, so a sample is on the order of one kilobyte, and thirty to seventy five samples is about thirty five to eighty five kilobytes a block. Against downloading the whole fifty two megabyte block that is roughly six hundred to fifteen hundred times less, and it is the same order as the erasure coding custody number, so per node availability is genuinely cheap. This is the piece whose foundation is already built, the erasure code from the erasure coding lever is its first component.

Two honest catches. First, the sampling only proves availability, that the data is there, it says nothing about validity, so it must be paired with one of the two validity answers above, it is not a substitute for either. Second, the crypto law shapes the construction. Danksharding style sampling on other chains uses a pairing based commitment that binds the erasure coding was done correctly for free, and pairings are banned here, so our sampling is Merkle based, which means the sample proofs are larger than a pairing scheme's and, more importantly, that the correctness of the encoding is not bound by the commitment and needs a fraud proof of incorrect coding or a two dimensional scheme to be safe. That is additional design, it is buildable and it stays inside the law, but it is not free and it is more than the one dimensional erasure code that exists today. The per node sampling cost stays small, the build to make it safe under the crypto law is real.

## What breaks

Byzantine trusted validity with sampling is cheap and buildable, the sampling is affordable and the validity inference is free, but it gives up independent re execution, so validity rests on each block's committee meeting the one third assumption with no second line behind it. Proven validity with sampling is trustless but the proving is not affordable at the target today and the proof lags finality by seconds. Either way the sampling itself needs the Merkle based two dimensional coding and its coding fraud proof to be safe under the crypto law, which is more than the one dimensional code that is built. And if the transaction stream is also split across the set, the cross shard atomicity and per shard security costs from the availability scope paper are added on top.

## The honest verdict

The availability half is affordable and real, about thirty five to eighty five kilobytes a node a block, six hundred to fifteen hundred times cheaper than downloading the block, and its foundation is built. The validity half is where the path is decided. Byzantine trusted validity is free in compute but trades away the independent re execution that catches a bad committee, so it is a security tradeoff and not a free win. Proven validity is the trustless answer and it is not affordable at a hundred thousand transactions a second today, the proving throughput does not exist on affordable hardware, the proof would lag finality by seconds, and it must be the post quantum STARK family and not a cheaper pairing proof. So the path can take the per validator download well below the one gigabit floor, but the honest reading is that the affordable version of it now is byzantine trusted, which buys the bandwidth by giving up independent re execution, and the trustless version is a multi year trajectory. It is a real path and it is the right direction, but it is not a free lever, and the trustless form of it is not something we can cost as affordable today. That is the plain answer, and it is the same shape as the erasure coding one, the cheap number is real and the thing everyone wants it to mean is the part that is not yet paid for.
